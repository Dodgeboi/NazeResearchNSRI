# AI Use Transparency Statement

This project used an AI assistant (Anthropic's Claude, via the Claude
Code tool) as a collaborator. In the interest of research integrity, we
describe that use honestly and completely.

## How AI was used

* **Code structure and design.** The AI helped design the repository
  layout, the module boundaries (network generator, propagation engine,
  defenses, optimizer, statistics, plotting), and the configuration
  schema.
* **Implementation and debugging.** The AI wrote initial versions of
  the simulation code and tests and helped debug them. The team ran the
  code, read it, and requested changes.
* **Documentation and writing organization.** The AI helped draft and
  organize the methodology, assumptions registry, data dictionary, this
  README, and the structure of the Research Brief, abstract,
  presentation, and Q&A materials.
* **Source formatting.** The AI helped format citations into a
  consistent style.

## What the AI did NOT do

* It did **not** invent any experimental result. Every number in the
  abstract and Research Brief is inserted automatically from the
  program's generated CSV files by `python -m grrc.cli report`; unfilled
  `{{TOKENS}}` are left visible rather than guessed.
* It did **not** fabricate citations. Each reference in
  `report/references.md` was opened and verified by a team member;
  anything unverifiable is marked `[SOURCE NEEDED]`.
* It did **not** independently decide the scientific claims. Hypotheses
  were written as predictions before the standard run
  (`docs/experiment_plan.md`), and conclusions are constrained to what
  the data show.

## Team responsibility and understanding

* Team members **reviewed and tested** all code and can explain how it
  works (see the cross-training checklist in `docs/team_plan.md`).
* We do **not** claim that we independently wrote code the AI generated;
  the AI's contribution to the implementation was substantial and is
  disclosed here.
* All experiment outputs were generated by running the program on our
  own machines with the documented seeds.
* Sources were manually verified by the team.
* No citations or results were knowingly fabricated.
* **The student team takes full responsibility for the submitted work,**
  including its correctness, its limitations, and its ethical framing.
* Every team member can explain the methods, the assumptions, the
  statistics, and — importantly — what the project does and does not
  prove.

## Reproducibility as a transparency mechanism

Because the entire study reruns from a clean checkout with fixed seeds
(`python scripts/reproduce_all.py --profile standard`), any reader —
judge, teacher, or peer — can independently regenerate every number and
figure and confirm that nothing was hand-edited. We consider this the
strongest possible evidence of integrity.
