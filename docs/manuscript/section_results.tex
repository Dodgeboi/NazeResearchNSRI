Heterogeneous tariff changes alter frontier membership in every profile
(Table~\ref{tab:weights}). A uniform conversion of units preserves dominance,
so varying a single common multiplier would have missed this dependence.
The result identifies sensitivity to declared assumptions; the chosen
perturbation interval does not supply an empirical distribution of costs.

The objective ablation also cautions against treating the total frontier
size as surprising by itself. Across two-objective subsets, frontier size
ranges from 1--16, 1--18, and 1--9 in the three profiles. Across all six
objectives it is 64, 22, and 9. However, tied single-objective minima can be
numerous: one intermediate objective has 47 tied efficient candidates.
The appendix reports every dimension. Frontier size depends on objective
choice, dependence, and ties; this study does not provide an independent-objective
null model that predicts 95 efficient candidates.

\subsection{What the assertion audit detects}
The preserved number/regex audit passes on the preserved manuscript despite
the three registered contradictions: the universal small-gap claim, the
discovery label on a confirmation figure, and the under-one-hour range.
The new assertions reject all \HistoricalContracts{} with witness values,
accept the \CorrectContracts{} correct cases, and reject or flag invalid
all \MutationContracts{} controlled mutations as specified. The latter
include a wrong stage, wrong unit, incomplete population, wrong aggregation,
wrong bound, strict-versus-inclusive boundary, missing population, and
unsupported operation. This is a finite regression demonstration. It
does not show that adding more assertions will discover an unknown omitted
claim or that a human translation of prose is correct.
