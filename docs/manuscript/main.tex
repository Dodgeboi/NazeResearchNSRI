\section{Introduction}
Hospital ransomware defense involves competing goals: reducing disruption
and extreme losses while controlling implementation cost and operational
burden. Incident studies document disruption \cite{neprash2022}, but those
observations do not identify what a particular hospital would gain from a
particular combination of controls. Simulation makes such counterfactuals
inspectable, provided its assumptions and decision implications are explicit.

We develop a synthetic hospital network model with pathway-specific defenses,
clinical-service dependencies, and paired scenario comparisons. Its evidence
framework distinguishes observed incident quantities, evidence-informed
context, declared technical assumptions, and normalized decision units.
This distinction prevents an adoption survey or a guidance document from
being treated as an estimate of control effectiveness. The finite experiment
enumerates 400 profile-candidates and compares six objectives without
collapsing them into a single preferred policy.

The central question is: \emph{when modeled outcomes change, which portfolio
decisions can still be defended?} Changing the service-outage definition can
substantially change event frequency while preserving the estimated efficient
set. Conversely, changing a restoration mechanism can alter that set even
when parameter-based ranking checks appear reassuring. These are different
forms of sensitivity, requiring different evidence.

We make three contributions. First, we provide an inspectable defense-portfolio
experiment that links evidence classification to mechanisms, matched scenarios,
and separate parameter and structural sensitivity analyses. Second, we derive
and implement a finite interval certificate that bounds every admissible
frontier between guaranteed and possible efficient sets, with competing
portfolios as failure witnesses. Applied to the endpoint interval, it certifies
retention of \CertGuaranteedTotal{} of the \CertObservedTotal{} observed
efficient portfolios. Third, we evaluate the certificate on two independently
defined engineering benchmarks and connect selected manuscript interpretations
to executable assertions and worked corrections.

Security portfolio simulation-optimization and necessary/possible efficiency
are established ideas \cite{kiesling2016,hladik2017}. Our contribution is their
concrete use to distinguish outcome sensitivity, frontier retention, and
remaining decision uncertainty in an evidence-constrained hospital experiment.
The added hospital analyses are retrospective. The benchmark evaluation plan
was committed before generating its candidate outcomes; the evaluations share
our research workflow and do not constitute independent clinical validation.

\section{Model and Evidence Design}
\subsection{Evidence constrains mechanisms, not every coefficient}
The parameter and endpoint registries separate evidence-constrained
quantities, external comparison targets, declared assumptions, model-internal
quantities, and normalized scenario units. A source must match both the
quantity and the population to identify a coefficient. Incident-level recovery
durations, for example, do not identify per-node restoration throughput.
Sector adoption figures can inform a scenario's context without estimating
the effectiveness of a deployed control.

This rule changes model structure. Patching mainly reduces exploit-mediated
traversal, has little effect on credential-mediated traversal, and has partial
effect on vendor pathways. Identity protection has explicit coverage and
effectiveness on eligible authentication pathways. Evidence about commercial
account authentication \cite{meyer2023} is not applied as a reduction in
whole-network ransomware probability. Isolated backups have no normal
traversal path, but can suffer a per-incident isolation lapse or residual
recovery failure. A small repeated traversal probability would instead model
a delay: over $T$ independent opportunities, a per-step probability $q$
produces cumulative probability $1-(1-q)^T$.

Technical effect sizes remain non-identifiable from the available public
sources. We sample \SampledCoefficients{} coefficients over declared ranges
once per scenario and share that vector across candidate replays. The
objectives therefore average over those ranges as well as event randomness.
Guidance motivates mechanisms; it does not supply invented numerical
effectiveness estimates. The complete register and mechanism specification
are included in the artifact.

\subsection{Network, objectives, and paired experiment}
One synthetic facility is a network of digital assets with four clinical-service
dependencies. Incidents enter through five categories: workstation,
internet-facing asset, privileged system, medical device, or vendor connection.
Spread, detection, defensive isolation, and restoration evolve in five-minute
steps over 72 hours. Each service is available when its declared 60\%
supporting-node threshold is met. This binary digital-service construct does
not measure patients treated or clinical harm. Weighted service-hours lost
sum unavailable time over seven services: EHR, laboratory, pharmacy, imaging,
scheduling, identity, and backup/recovery, with declared weights
$(1.0,0.8,0.9,0.7,0.4,0.9,0.6)$. Only the first four enter the clinical-outage
endpoint; supporting-service losses still contribute to total weighted loss.

The resource-constrained, intermediate, and high-capacity profiles are fixed
environments with 288, 96, and 16 distinct resolved candidates. Controls vary
segmentation, patching, detection, isolation, backups, and identity protection;
they cannot purchase a different profile. Each candidate has six minimized
objectives: mean weighted service-hours lost, empirical upper-tail mean loss,
sustained-outage frequency, non-recovery by 72 hours, implementation cost,
and operational burden. The tail statistic averages losses at or above the
empirical 90th percentile, including ties. Cost and burden are normalized
scenario points, not monetary or staffing estimates.

Discovery contains 30 scenarios per profile and 12,000 candidate executions.
Confirmation contains \RevisionScenarios{} fresh scenarios per profile and
\RevisionExecutions{} executions. Disjoint scenario-ID blocks separate
profiles and stages; within a profile, all candidates share event-keyed
random inputs. Confirmation cycles through five entry categories, giving
80 scenarios per category. The estimand weights those categories equally.
The independent units are the scenarios, not the individual candidate
executions, and the designed mixture is not hospital incident prevalence.

\section{Decision Stability and Verification}
\subsection{Outcome definitions and population alignment}
Let $N_{pci}$ be the number of clinical services with an individual continuous
outage longer than two hours in profile $p$, candidate $c$, scenario $i$.
The event $Y^{(k)}_{pci}=\mathbf{1}\{N_{pci}\geq k\}$ requires at least $k$
qualifying services; their outage intervals need not overlap. The primary
endpoint uses $k=\PrimaryK$. We report all four values of $k$ and recompute the candidate-average frequency at each
$k\in\{1,2,3,4\}$ separately for each stage and profile. Averaging over
candidates describes this enumerated design, not an incident prevalence.

For the same observations and weights, the following identity holds exactly:
\begin{equation}
 \overline{Y^{(1)}}-\overline{Y^{(4)}}
 = \overline{\mathbf{1}\{1\leq N\leq 3\}}. \label{eq:identity}
\end{equation}
The fraction of partial-service outcomes is therefore the endpoint's
$k=1$ versus $k=4$ sensitivity, not independent evidence for its insensitivity.
The identity does not justify moving a pooled discovery statistic into a
profile-specific confirmation claim. We check the identity numerically and
report both candidate-weighted pooling and equal-profile averaging.

\subsection{Pairing, allocation, and uncertainty}
For two candidates, define the paired loss difference $D_{hi}$ in entry
stratum $h$, with allocation weight $w_h=n_h/n$. The estimated mean-difference
variance is
\begin{equation}
 \widehat{\mathrm{Var}}(\widehat{\Delta})
 = \sum_{h=1}^{5} w_h^2\frac{s^2_{D,h}}{n_h},\qquad
 s^2_{D,h}=s^2_{a,h}+s^2_{b,h}-2s_{ab,h}. \label{eq:pairing}
\end{equation}
Dropping covariance changes the question's uncertainty. It can either inflate
or deflate an estimate depending on covariance; pairing is not guaranteed to
help for every possible model. For the nonlinear tail statistic, covariance
is estimated from shared stratified bootstrap draws.

We compare paired and covariance-omitting versions of the earlier
``within one standard error on all four stochastic objectives'' diagnostic,
using the same design and bootstrap draws. This is a descriptive heuristic,
not a hypothesis test, equivalence test, or proof that portfolios can be ordered.
For a more explicit mean-loss comparison, every nonfree candidate is compared
with its own profile's free baseline. Approximate paired t intervals use
Satterthwaite degrees of freedom and Bonferroni adjustment across all
\ContrastFamily{} contrasts. This includes the observed best candidate in
the family rather than presenting it as a prespecified isolated comparison.
Zero observed within-stratum variance is marked unestimable, not certain.

\subsection{Decision-set and specification diagnostics}
A candidate is Pareto efficient if no other candidate is no worse on every
included objective and strictly better on at least one. We enumerate the
full finite space; no optimization heuristic approximates this set.
Let $F(X)$ denote the frontier in candidate set $X$, and let $S$ be the 65
candidates labeled finalists by the frozen discovery rule. We distinguish
\emph{omissions}, $F(X)\setminus S$, from \emph{restricted-view artifacts},
$F(S)\setminus F(X)$. A shortlist can omit efficient candidates without
containing any spurious efficient candidate itself.

We resample whole scenarios within each entry category, preserving all
candidate and endpoint pairings. Each of \BootstrapDraws{} draws recomputes
the six objectives and both frontiers. Independent profile blocks are
resampled separately. Finalist labels remain fixed: the analysis conditions
on the observed discovery decision and does not rerun its selection rule.
Percentile summaries are conditional resampling ranges, not confidence sets
for a uniquely true frontier or forecasts for other hospitals. Discrete
dominance and selection make ordinary bootstrap coverage especially uncertain.

We also enumerate all 63 nonempty subsets of the six objectives and compare
frontier membership across $k$. Jaccard similarity is intersection size divided
by union size. Finally, \WeightDraws{} stress draws independently multiply
each of eight cost and eight burden coefficients by a Uniform$(0.5,1.5)$
factor, shared across profiles. Draws reversing the cumulative order of the
segmentation or backup tariffs are rejected (\WeightRejected{} rejections).
These are analyst-declared perturbations, not empirical priors. Positive
uniform scaling of cost and burden is checked separately: it preserves
Pareto dominance algebraically, although keeping a numeric budget fixed can
change affordability.


\subsection{An interval certificate for frontier retention}
Let $f_c\in\mathbb{R}^m$ be candidate $c$'s minimized objective vector, with
componentwise bounds $L_c\leq f_c\leq U_c$. Write $a\prec b$ when $a$ is no
worse in every coordinate and strictly better in at least one. The finite
Cartesian box $\mathcal{B}$ permits independent choices across candidates
and objectives. Define
\begin{align}
 G &= \{c:\nexists d\ne c,\ L_d\prec U_c\}, \label{eq:guaranteed}\\
 P &= \{c:\nexists d\ne c,\ U_d\prec L_c\}. \label{eq:possible}
\end{align}
\textbf{Proposition.} $G=\bigcap_{f\in\mathcal B}F(f)$ and
$P=\bigcup_{f\in\mathcal B}F(f)$. Consequently,
$G\subseteq F(f)\subseteq P$ for every admissible matrix.

\emph{Proof.} If $L_d\prec U_c$, choosing $d$ at its lower bound and $c$
at its upper bound makes $c$ dominated. Conversely, any realized dominator
implies $L_d\prec U_c$, so its absence guarantees efficiency. If
$U_d\prec L_c$, $d$ dominates $c$ in every realization. If no such $d$
exists, put $c$ at $L_c$ and every competitor at its upper bound:
none dominates $c$, proving possible efficiency. Independence permits these
simultaneous corner assignments. Strictness retains exact ties. $\square$

The algorithm requires $O(n^2m)$ comparisons and returns a competing
candidate for each failed guarantee or excluded possibility. These are
finite-box instances of established necessary/possible efficiency concepts
\cite{hladik2017}. We use them as an interpretable diagnostic, without a
probabilistic coverage claim. If the true specification set is a correlated
subset of $\mathcal B$, $G$ remains sufficient for retention and $P$ remains
an outer bound; they need not be exact for that smaller set.

For the hospital application, five objectives remain fixed at their observed
confirmation estimates. The outage coordinate of each candidate ranges
independently from its $k=4$ frequency to its $k=1$ frequency. This contains
all four common-$k$ specifications and additional combinations that a common
threshold cannot produce. Certifying a candidate across this entire box
is stronger than observing it at four thresholds. An uncertified candidate
need not actually change membership under those four thresholds.

\subsection{Parameter, structure, and external transfer checks}
The preserved parameter analysis compares candidate rankings between the
low and high thirds of each sampled coefficient. Separately, the structural
experiment contrasts restoration gated on containment with restoration
proceeding in parallel. It uses a distinct paired bank of \StructuralTrials{}
scenarios per profile and \StructuralCandidates{} selected candidates
(frontier members, comparators, and preference selections), rather than
reenumerating the entire space. Membership comparisons are relative to that
selected set and bank.

For transfer beyond the hospital objectives, we use the published RE21
four-bar truss and RE22 reinforced-concrete beam implementations
\cite{tanabe2020}. We archive the unchanged upstream code, commit, hashes,
and license. A committed evaluation plan specifies 128 candidates sampled
uniformly over each problem's variable bounds, and independent objective
intervals of $\pm1\%$, $\pm5\%$, and $\pm10\%$ of each sampled objective
range, with nonnegative lower bounds. RE22 retains its published constraint
violation objective; not every sampled design is feasible.

Each of the six settings has 1,000 interior perturbation matrices. A separate
two-objective sorting oracle checks frontier inclusion between $G$ and $P$.
For the first eight candidates per problem, a second check enumerates all
256 corners with only the second objective uncertain at 5\% bounds, requiring
exact agreement with the frontier intersection and union. These checks
evaluate implementation and transfer to fixed finite benchmark sets.
The stress bounds are not measured engineering uncertainty.

\subsection{Executable interpretation assertions}
The registry gives each selected claim a source table, scope filter, expected
groups and row count, statistic, unit, reduction, quantifier, relation, and
bound. For example, interpreting a claim that all profile gaps are roughly
three percentage points as $\max_p\Delta_p\leq4$ percentage points requires
the confirmation population and every profile. Four is a generous audit
tolerance for that wording, not a clinical cutoff. Failed assertions return
group-specific witness values. Missing populations, unit mismatches, and
unsupported operations are invalid and stop verification.

The finite evaluation has \CorrectContracts{} correct assertions,
\HistoricalContracts{} historical contradictions, and \MutationContracts{}
single-field mutations of correct assertions. It includes exact-boundary,
missing-population, and unsupported-operation cases. These were constructed
after reviewing the paper. Detection on this set demonstrates the specified
checks, not sensitivity or specificity on unseen manuscripts. A human must
still judge whether an assertion captures the intended sentence and whether
the source itself has scientific validity.

\section{Reproduction and Audit Boundary}
The original raw banks are unchanged. New analysis starts from a committed,
clean source state captured before output generation. Each manifest records
the source commit, environment, input and output sizes, and SHA-256 hashes.
The historical generated-number manifest records a dirty working tree; we
preserve that fact. Hash agreement verifies artifact consistency but cannot
retroactively prove that the historical code was committed before execution.
A commit-attribution map resolves historical source identifiers after the
repository's metadata-only attribution correction.

For the retrospective analysis and benchmark checks, Python 3.12 and exact package versions
are recorded in the manifest and revision environment file. The reproduction
guide gives commands to restore the compressed bank, run the verification
harness, regenerate the analyses and benchmark checks from clean checkouts,
and generate numeric TeX and vector figures after committing the result tables. The paper
generator's check mode recomputes the TeX and verifies its manifest without
overwriting files. These checks certify specified artifact relations, not
the correctness of every sentence.

The retrospective plan records an amendment: early diagnostic output treated
entry categories as randomly mixed, but source inspection established a
fixed allocation. Final results preserve that allocation. Initial outputs
are retained in Git history rather than relabeled as the final design.
For Eq.~\ref{eq:pairing}, set $v_h=w_h^2s^2_{D,h}/n_h$ and use approximate
degrees of freedom $(\sum_hv_h)^2/\sum_h[v_h^2/(n_h-1)]$. The interval is
$\widehat\Delta\pm t_{1-.05/(2\cdot\ContrastFamily),\nu}\sqrt{\sum_hv_h}$.
