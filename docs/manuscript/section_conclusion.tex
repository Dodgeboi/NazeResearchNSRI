We rebuilt a synthetic hospital ransomware decision model after an audit
found that its prose and its implementation described different models. The
sustained-outage endpoint was defined one way and computed another; capacity
profiles were overwritten by portfolios at zero cost; the reported frontier
ranged over a discovery-selected subset; and the protocol that claimed to
have frozen that subset rewrote itself on every run. A seventy-test suite
passed throughout.

The rebuilt study evaluates every resolved candidate on a fresh paired
scenario bank under a content-addressed protocol committed before any result
existed. \ConfirmFullSpaceFrontier{} portfolios are non-dominated across six
separately reported objectives, and none minimizes all of them. Because the
comparison is full-space, we can also report what the previous design could
not: \ConfirmFinalistOnlyArtifacts{} candidates that look efficient only
inside the restricted subset, and \ConfirmNonFinalistsOnFrontier{} efficient
candidates it would never have shown.

The findings that matter for a reader are negative ones. In the strongest
modeled environment no purchasable control makes a detectable difference to
disruption. In the weakest, the declared budget does not reach the outcome
the endpoint measures. And of \ValidationBenchmarks{} frozen external
benchmarks, only \ValidationScored{} can be scored at all --- the model has
no output matching the rest --- while \ValidationFailed{} fail and none
passes. The restoration engine is simultaneously too slow for the one
observed large-scale technical outage and too fast for the ransomware
incident record, and the model assigns probability zero to a quarter of
observed multi-hospital events.

The defensible contribution is therefore not a defense portfolio and not a
frontier. It is a demonstration that the honest versions of three
methodological choices --- evaluating the full candidate space, freezing a
protocol in a way a reader can check, and reporting external validation
failures as results --- are affordable, and that each converts an assumption
a study would otherwise have to make about itself into a quantity it can
report. The outputs support research and tabletop exploration \modelonly.
They should not be used as hospital risk estimates, procurement
recommendations, or evidence that a deployed control will achieve the
reported hours or probabilities.
