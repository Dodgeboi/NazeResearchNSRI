Simulation reporting guidelines, especially STRESS, already call for
transparent model descriptions, experimental design, and implementation
details \cite{monks2019}. Preregistration distinguishes prior commitments
from outcome-informed analysis \cite{nosek2018}. Our retrospective plan and
amendment use that distinction explicitly; recording them does not transform
an audit informed by observed defects into a confirmatory experiment.

Common random numbers are an established variance-reduction strategy.
Event-keyed random inputs can preserve meaningful coupling when alternative
policies change execution order \cite{buffalo2026}. This study does not
introduce that technique. Its additional observation is that a paired
simulator can still be followed by an uncertainty calculation that discards
the resulting covariance. The analysis must also retain fixed design strata;
correct pairing alone is insufficient.

Multiverse and specification-curve approaches expose variation across
defensible analytical choices \cite{steegen2016, simonsohn2020}. Our endpoint,
objective-subset, and tariff exercises are narrower descriptive diagnostics.
We do not implement the joint inference of a full specification-curve
analysis or claim that every enumerated specification has equal substantive
merit. Their purpose is to separate endpoint-frequency sensitivity from
decision-set sensitivity within one finite experiment.

Automated statistical consistency checks such as statcheck address whether
reported test statistics and p-values agree \cite{nuijten2016}. Metamorphic
testing examines expected relations when direct simulation oracles are
unavailable \cite{raunak2021}. Our endpoint identity and unit-scaling check
are examples of this broader relational-testing tradition. The small claim
registry adds explicit population, units, and quantifiers for selected
interpretations; it does not replace either numerical or model validation.

A recent preprint, ABE-Ralph, represents experimental claims and protocols
as structured constraints and checks fidelity in LLM-driven scientific
workflows \cite{yu2026}. It is directly relevant prior work, so a broad claim
to be the first executable scientific-claim auditor would be unwarranted.
Our narrower focus is retrospective statistical interpretation of a security
simulation, including paired covariance, fixed entry allocation, and
conditional Pareto-set diagnostics. Unlike a general claim-extraction
system, our evaluator only handles explicitly authored assertions. The
assertions are useful because their assumptions and failure witnesses are
small enough to inspect, not because they automate scientific judgment.

Finally, healthcare cyber-disruption models can address process overload
using observations and expert assessment \cite{willing2025}. Our case lacks
independent clinical validation and cannot compete on predictive realism.
Its research value lies in exposing errors that could also occur in a more
realistic model: a valid computation can still be summarized with the wrong
population or strength of conclusion.
