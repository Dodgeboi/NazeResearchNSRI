Kiesling et al.\ combine security-control models, multi-objective
simulation-optimization, and portfolio selection \cite{kiesling2016}.
Hlad\'ik studies efficiency under interval uncertainty \cite{hladik2017}.
Our finite-box formulas are direct specializations of that established
idea. Their role is to make a limited stability claim explicit and
computable; the shared-price calculation preserves the dependency
structure of the portfolio cost model.

Simulation ranking and selection also has a substantial literature.
Currie and Monks's BootComp uses bootstrap comparisons, permits common
random numbers, and includes a hospital ward application \cite{currie2021}.
Andrad\'ottir and Lee develop Pareto-set selection procedures with guaranteed
correct-selection probabilities under independent or correlated sampling
\cite{andradottir2021}. Our fixed-bank study does not propose a replacement
selection algorithm. It compares deterministic retention, conditional
resampling frequency, and a conservative simultaneous mean screen on
the same candidate set.

The design follows the transparency goals of STRESS reporting
\cite{monks2019}, common-random-number practice \cite{buffalo2026},
and explicit separation of prior plans from retrospective analysis
\cite{nosek2018}. Multiverse and specification-curve studies motivate
examining analytical choices \cite{steegen2016,simonsohn2020};
our study does not implement their joint inference procedures.
Statistical consistency checks \cite{nuijten2016}, metamorphic simulation
tests \cite{raunak2021}, and the ABE-Ralph experimental-fidelity preprint
\cite{yu2026} provide context for the interpretation assertions.
The scientific addition is the documented change in conclusions when
several assumptions are examined together, supported by an auditable
implementation and public construct evidence.
