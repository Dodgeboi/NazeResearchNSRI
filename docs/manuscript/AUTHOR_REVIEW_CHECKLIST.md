# Author review checklist

Do not submit until all three authors can check every box.

## Scientific understanding

- [ ] We can explain the research question without reading the abstract.
- [ ] We understand what one node, edge, step, scenario, and portfolio mean.
- [ ] We can explain common random numbers and why comparisons are paired.
- [ ] We understand weighted service-hours lost and the 60% support rule.
- [ ] We understand why the 60% rule and two-hour indicator are definitions,
      not clinical standards.
- [ ] We can explain why public incident papers cannot identify a universal
      per-edge spread rate or control-effectiveness probability.
- [ ] We can explain the time-step sensitivity and why the paper emphasizes
      direction more than magnitude.

## Claims and numbers

- [ ] We checked every number in the abstract against the results tables.
- [ ] We checked every table and figure against the archived CSV files.
- [ ] We read the cited papers used for the main factual claims.
- [ ] We agree that the manuscript never calls simulated output real-world
      risk, harm, efficacy, or probability.
- [ ] We agree with the stated limitations and stop point.

## Authorship and disclosure

- [ ] Author names, order, affiliation, and contact details are correct.
- [ ] The author-contribution statement accurately reflects who did what.
- [ ] The AI-use statement accurately reflects Claude, ChatGPT, Codex, or any
      other tools used by each author.
- [ ] Every author has rewritten any sentence that does not sound like them.
- [ ] Every author approves the final manuscript and takes responsibility for
      it.

## Submission readiness

- [ ] A teacher, mentor, or statistics reviewer has reviewed the study.
- [ ] Hospital IT/clinical-operations face validation is completed or the
      target venue explicitly accepts an unvalidated methodological study.
- [ ] The target journal or competition format has been applied.
- [ ] The final code/data release has been checked for sensitive information.
- [ ] The protocol history and documented diagnostic correction remain public.
