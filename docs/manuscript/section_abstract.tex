Cyber-defense portfolios trade service continuity against implementation
cost, operational burden, and residual tail risk, and public evidence
identifies almost none of the coefficients a simulation of them requires. We
report a synthetic, partially calibrated multi-objective decision analysis
that classifies every quantity by what identifies it and reports the
unidentified ones as declared assumptions rather than estimates.

This version corrects its own predecessor, whose defects we report rather
than supersede: an endpoint defined in prose as requiring four clinical
services and computed as requiring one; capacity profiles that portfolios
could overwrite at zero cost; a frontier reported over a discovery-selected
subset; and a protocol that rewrote its own claim to have been frozen. A
seventy-test suite passed throughout, because no test asserted a definition.

The rebuilt study evaluates every resolved candidate ---
\ConfirmResourceConstrainedCandidates{}, \ConfirmIntermediateCandidates{},
and \ConfirmHighCapacityCandidates{} across three capacity profiles --- on
fresh paired scenarios under a content-addressed protocol committed before
any result existed, for \ConfirmTotalExecutions{} executions. Scenario counts
were set per profile from a pilot precision analysis rather than declared.
\ConfirmFullSpaceFrontier{} candidates are non-dominated on six separately
reported objectives and none minimizes all six. Because the comparison is
full-space we also report what a restricted holdout cannot:
\ConfirmFinalistOnlyArtifacts{} candidates efficient only within the
discovery-selected subset, and \ConfirmNonFinalistsOnFrontier{} efficient
candidates that subset would never have shown.

The informative results are negative. In the strongest profile the entire
purchasable improvement is \ConfirmHighCapacityMaxPurchasableImprovement{}
weighted service-hours and a third of candidate pairs are statistically
indistinguishable. In the weakest, the declared budget leaves sustained
clinical-outage probability at
\ConfirmResourceConstrainedBestAffordableOutage\%. Of \ValidationBenchmarks{}
frozen external benchmarks only \ValidationScored{} can be scored,
\ValidationFailed{} fail, and none passes. Every number here is generated
from a committed results table by script, and a claim audit fails the build
when prose and artifacts disagree. The framework supports auditable
trade-off exploration \modelonly; it does not estimate hospital risk or
control effectiveness.
