Choosing defenses from a security simulation requires knowing which results
depend on its assumptions. We develop an evidence-constrained synthetic
hospital ransomware model with pathway-specific controls, six decision
objectives, and \RevisionExecutions{} confirmation executions paired across
400 profile-candidates. We separate parameter uncertainty, structural choices,
outcome definitions, and efficient-set membership. Changing the outage-service
threshold shifts high-capacity candidate-average event frequency by
\HighGap{} percentage points while preserving its observed Pareto frontier.
A finite interval certificate goes further: \CertGuaranteedTotal{} of
\CertObservedTotal{} observed efficient portfolios remain efficient throughout
candidate-specific endpoint intervals, with five other objectives fixed.
All \CertHighGuaranteed{} high-capacity frontier members are retained,
although \CertHighAdditional{} additional candidates remain possibly efficient.
Published truss and beam benchmarks provide transfer checks over
\BenchmarkDraws{} perturbations and \BenchmarkCorners{} exact corner matrices;
wide bounds expose when certification becomes inconclusive.
Paired stratified analysis, structural sensitivity, and executable
interpretation assertions complete the evaluation. The contribution is an
inspectable model and a decision-stability analysis that distinguishes
guaranteed retention from observed agreement. Its guarantees concern the
declared objective bounds, not clinical effectiveness.
