Reproducing simulation numbers does not guarantee that a paper interprets
them correctly. We audit one synthetic hospital ransomware study whose
software checks and generated manuscript values coexisted with incorrect
scope and uncertainty claims. Without changing the simulator or its
\RevisionExecutions{} confirmation executions, we reanalyze paired scenarios,
preserve the fixed allocation across attack-entry categories, and check
selected interpretations with explicit executable assertions. Changing the
required outage-service count shifts the high-capacity profile's
candidate-average event frequency by \HighGap{} percentage points while
leaving its estimated six-objective Pareto frontier unchanged. A frozen
discovery shortlist omits \OmittedCandidates{} efficient candidates in the
observed bank; the conditional stratified-bootstrap range is
\OmittedLow{}--\OmittedHigh{}. Although the observed shortlist contains no
restricted-view artifacts, such artifacts appear in \ArtifactPositive{}\%
of resamples. Covariance-aware uncertainty also contradicts a blanket
interpretation of no detectable control benefit. Our contribution is a
retrospective security-experimentation case study linking checkable claims
to population, pairing, and decision-set diagnostics. It provides neither a
general natural-language validator nor validated hospital recommendations.
