An unchanged Pareto frontier can make a simulation look more robust than it
is. We examine this problem in a single synthetic hospital model with
pathway-specific defenses, six objectives, and \RevisionExecutions{} paired
confirmation executions across 400 profile-candidates. Changing the clinical
outage definition moves high-capacity event frequency by \HighGap{}
percentage points without changing its observed frontier. An interval
calculation retains \CertGuaranteedTotal{} of \CertObservedTotal{} efficient
portfolios when only that endpoint varies. We then let shared component
prices vary and recompute the stochastic objectives through paired,
entry-stratified resampling. At $\pm50\%$ prices, even requiring every
upgrade to retain half its nominal incremental price reduces guaranteed
retention to \JointRetainedFloored{}. Only \JointFrequentFloored{} of the
original certificates persist in at least 95\% of resamples. A conservative
simultaneous bound on population means supports only the free baselines.
An external construct audit of \CipherRecords{} public clinical-harm records
also identifies services and time periods outside the model. The contribution
is a reproducible joint stability study with an exact shared-price retention
calculation and explicit separation of deterministic guarantees, resampling
frequencies, and statistical confidence. The results identify which assumptions
deserve scrutiny before using the simulated tradeoffs to inform a decision.
